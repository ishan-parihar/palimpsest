# Discrimination probes

Loaded after `[RELEASE]`, before `[CLAIM]`.

Recognition of a framework is worthless. Knowing when a framework **does not
apply** is most of the value, and it is the half nobody counts. A tool the user
has never correctly declined is indistinguishable from one they have never had.

## The probes

Run one. Not all. Choose the one that would actually hurt if it were missing.

**Negative case.** "Here is a situation that looks like the one you just
analysed. Argue that your answer does not apply." Make it genuinely tempting and
genuinely wrong for their framework. If they cannot break it, they have
recognition, not discrimination.

**Tool-confusion.** "You reached for <X>. Was that a bottleneck problem or a
feedback problem? What observation would tell them apart?" Most real failures
are the wrong tool confidently applied, not a missing tool.

**Reversal.** "Now the same situation, one condition flipped." The atlas's own
gate: predict the eighteen-month behaviour your first analysis would have got
wrong. If both analyses agree, nothing was modelled.

**Overreach.** "Where does this framework stop working? Name the boundary
explicitly, not a caveat." A framework applied past its ceiling is worse than no
framework, because it is load-bearing and wrong.

**Disconfirming case.** "Argue yourself out of the framework you just used." If
the user cannot find the real objection, they have not understood it well enough
to be protected by it.

**The silent one.** Present a situation with no framing at all. Do not name a
situation type, do not signal that a framework is available. The correct answer
is often "nothing here is a fit, and here is why." If the user reaches for a
framework anyway, they are pattern-matching to your availability, not to the
world.

## Scoring

There is no scoring. Do not say the probe was passed. Do not grade it. Do not
compare it to the last one.

If the user breaks their own framework, that is the work. Say nothing about it.
Move to `[CLAIM]`.

If they cannot, do not rescue them. Name the specific missing piece and stop.
The gap is the lesson and filling it for them spends it.
